What happened

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has decided to keep the repo rate unchanged, maintaining its current monetary policy stance. Simultaneously, the central bank has projected India's real GDP growth for the current fiscal year at 6.9%, signaling a focus on balancing inflation control with economic expansion.

India / market angle

This decision directly influences borrowing costs for Indian businesses and consumers, affecting credit growth and domestic consumption. It also impacts foreign investor sentiment regarding the stability of the Indian rupee and the attractiveness of Indian debt markets in a high-global-interest-rate environment.

Federal Reserve Implements First Interest Rate Hike Since 2023

Global economy & central banks · ABC News - Breaking News, Latest News and Videos · read original ↗

What happened

The U.S. Federal Reserve has raised interest rates for the first time since 2023, citing persistent inflationary pressures and stronger-than-expected economic growth. This move marks a shift in the central bank's strategy as it attempts to cool the economy without triggering a recession.

India / market angle

A higher U.S. interest rate regime typically leads to capital outflows from emerging markets like India as investors seek higher yields in dollar-denominated assets. This puts downward pressure on the INR, increases the cost of external commercial borrowings for Indian firms, and forces the RBI to maintain a tighter policy stance to defend the currency.

What to watch

The upcoming U.S. Consumer Price Index (CPI) data and the Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) dot plot to gauge the trajectory of future rate hikes.
