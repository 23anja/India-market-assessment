US Federal Reserve Holds Interest Rates Steady Amid Persistent Inflation

Global economy & central banks · CNBC · read original ↗

What happened

The U.S. Federal Reserve has maintained its benchmark interest rate, citing the need for more data to confirm that inflation is sustainably moving toward its 2% target. Officials emphasized that they remain cautious about cutting rates too soon, despite some signs of economic cooling. The decision reflects a 'wait-and-see' approach as the central bank balances labor market strength against stubborn price pressures.

India / market angle

For India, a 'higher-for-longer' U.S. rate environment exerts pressure on the INR, as the interest rate differential between the U.S. and India narrows, potentially triggering foreign portfolio outflows. It also limits the Reserve Bank of India's (RBI) room to maneuver on domestic rate cuts, as the RBI must maintain a buffer to prevent excessive currency depreciation and imported inflation.

What to watch

Upcoming U.S. monthly CPI and non-farm payroll data, which will serve as the primary indicators for the Fed's next policy shift.

China Expands Export Controls on Critical Technology and Minerals

China & global trade · Reuters · read original ↗

What happened

China has implemented new, stringent export controls on key technologies and minerals, framing the move as a national security measure. These restrictions target sectors critical to advanced manufacturing, including AI, semiconductors, and rare earth processing. The policy is widely viewed as a strategic counter-measure to Western-led trade restrictions and technology sanctions.

India / market angle

India's 'Make in India' initiative, particularly in electronics and renewable energy manufacturing, remains heavily dependent on Chinese supply chains for raw materials and components. These curbs threaten to increase input costs for Indian manufacturers and force a faster, albeit expensive, pivot toward alternative sourcing or domestic production of critical minerals.

What to watch

The specific list of minerals or technologies added to the restricted list and the subsequent impact on global commodity prices and Indian industrial procurement costs.

RBI Projects Decline in India's Public Debt-to-GDP Ratio by 2032

India economy & policy · Livemint · read original ↗

What happened

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has projected that India's public debt-to-GDP ratio will decline to 75.1% by the fiscal year 2032. This forecast positions India as the only major emerging economy expected to see a sustained reduction in its debt burden over the coming decade. The projection assumes consistent fiscal consolidation and sustained GDP growth exceeding 6-7%.

India / market angle

A declining debt-to-GDP ratio improves India's sovereign credit profile, potentially leading to lower borrowing costs for the government and private sector over the long term. This fiscal discipline provides the RBI with greater policy credibility and helps stabilize the rupee, making Indian sovereign bonds more attractive to global institutional investors.

What to watch

Annual Union Budget announcements and fiscal deficit targets, which will serve as the primary benchmarks to verify if the government remains on this consolidation path.
