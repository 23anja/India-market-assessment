Rising Iran-related war risks increase tanker freight costs for India

Global geopolitics · ThePrint · read original ↗

What happened

Escalating geopolitical tensions in the Middle East are driving up maritime insurance premiums and freight rates for oil tankers. Even alternative shipping routes are seeing cost pressures as insurers adjust risk assessments for vessels operating in or near conflict-prone zones.

India / market angle

India is highly dependent on crude oil imports, much of which transit through the Middle East. Higher freight costs directly inflate the landed cost of oil, putting upward pressure on domestic fuel prices, increasing the import bill, and potentially widening the current account deficit.

What to watch

Monitor the Baltic Dirty Tanker Index and any official statements from the Ministry of Petroleum regarding changes in insurance surcharges for Indian-bound crude shipments.

Bank of Japan raises interest rates to highest level since 1995

Global economy & central banks · Financial Times · read original ↗

What happened

The Bank of Japan has hiked interest rates to 1.25%, marking a significant departure from its long-standing ultra-loose monetary policy. This move reflects the central bank's response to persistent domestic inflation and a desire to stabilize the yen.

India / market angle

The end of Japan's negative interest rate era impacts global capital flows, specifically the 'yen carry trade' where investors borrow cheaply in yen to invest in higher-yielding emerging markets like India. A stronger yen and higher Japanese rates may lead to a reallocation of global liquidity, potentially tightening foreign institutional investment (FII) inflows into Indian equities.

What to watch

Watch for volatility in the USD/INR and JPY/INR exchange rates and any shifts in FII net buying patterns in Indian debt and equity markets.

Moody’s upgrades India’s FY27 GDP growth forecast to 7%

India economy & policy · The Economic Times · read original ↗

What happened

Moody’s Investors Service has revised its growth projection for India for the 2026-27 fiscal year to 7%, citing a resilient domestic economy and robust investment activity. The agency highlighted that despite global headwinds, India's internal demand remains a strong driver of growth.

India / market angle

This upgrade serves as a positive signal for international investors, potentially lowering the cost of capital for Indian firms and supporting sovereign credit ratings. Sustained growth at this level is crucial for maintaining the current momentum in domestic capital expenditure and attracting long-term foreign direct investment (FDI).

What to watch

Watch for upcoming quarterly GDP data releases and RBI commentary on whether actual domestic consumption matches these optimistic investment-led growth forecasts.
