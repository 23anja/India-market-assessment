Federal Reserve Maintains Interest Rates Amid Persistent Inflation

What happened

The U.S. Federal Reserve has opted to hold interest rates steady as inflation remains at a three-year high. This decision reflects the central bank's cautious approach to balancing price stability with economic growth in a volatile environment.

India / market angle

For India, a 'higher-for-longer' U.S. interest rate environment puts pressure on the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to maintain its own hawkish stance to prevent capital flight and currency depreciation. It also impacts the cost of external commercial borrowings for Indian corporates and increases the yield pressure on Indian government bonds.

What to watch

Upcoming U.S. Consumer Price Index (CPI) data and Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting minutes for signals on the timing of potential rate cuts.

India economy & policy · News On AIR · read original ↗

What happened

The Reserve Bank of India has maintained the repo rate, prioritizing inflation control while signaling confidence in the domestic economy. The central bank projects real GDP growth at 6.9% for the current fiscal year, balancing growth momentum with price stability.

India / market angle

This decision provides stability to the Indian banking sector and keeps borrowing costs for retail and corporate loans unchanged. For investors, it signals a focus on long-term macro-stability over short-term stimulus, which is crucial for maintaining foreign portfolio investment inflows.

What to watch

Future RBI Monetary Policy Committee (MPC) commentary regarding food inflation volatility and its impact on the timing of potential rate easing.

European Manufacturing Faces Significant Job Cuts Amid Supply Chain Shifts

China & global trade · The Guardian · read original ↗

What happened

European industrial sectors are warning of up to 300,000 job losses as they struggle to compete with Chinese supply chains. Industry leaders cite structural disadvantages and the aggressive expansion of Chinese manufacturing as the primary drivers of this industrial decline.

India / market angle

This shift represents a strategic opportunity for India's 'Make in India' initiative. As European firms seek to diversify supply chains away from China (de-risking), India can position itself as an alternative manufacturing hub, particularly in sectors like chemicals, automotive components, and light engineering.

What to watch

Policy announcements from the EU regarding industrial subsidies or trade protectionism that could either hinder or accelerate the relocation of manufacturing capacity to alternative markets like India.
