Surging Oil Shipping Costs Due to Middle East Conflict

Global geopolitics · BusinessLine · read original ↗

What happened

The ongoing conflict in the Middle East has caused oil shipping costs to spike sevenfold, with rates exceeding $20 per barrel. This surge is driven by increased insurance premiums and the need for tankers to avoid high-risk maritime routes, disrupting global energy logistics.

India / market angle

As the world's third-largest oil importer, India is highly sensitive to landed costs of crude. Higher shipping costs directly inflate the import bill, putting pressure on the current account deficit and the Indian Rupee (INR), while potentially forcing domestic oil marketing companies to either absorb costs or pass them on to consumers via higher pump prices.

What to watch

Monitor the monthly trade deficit data and any government announcements regarding adjustments to the windfall tax on domestic crude production.

Market Consensus Shifts to Federal Reserve Rate Hike in December

Global economy & central banks · Reuters · read original ↗

What happened

Economists overwhelmingly expect the U.S. Federal Reserve to pause interest rate hikes in October but implement another increase in December. This outlook is driven by persistent inflation and economic data that suggests the Fed's tightening cycle may need to continue longer than previously anticipated.

India / market angle

A higher-for-longer interest rate environment in the U.S. strengthens the dollar and triggers capital outflows from emerging markets like India as investors seek higher risk-free yields in US Treasuries. This creates a challenging environment for the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) to manage INR volatility and domestic liquidity without stifling credit growth.

What to watch

The upcoming U.S. Personal Consumption Expenditures (PCE) price index and the subsequent Federal Open Market Committee (FOMC) meeting minutes.

China's Export Restrictions on Tunnel Boring Machines (TBMs)

China & global trade · Outlook India · read original ↗

What happened

China has implemented export controls on Tunnel Boring Machines, a critical component for large-scale infrastructure projects. These restrictions come as global supply chains face increasing scrutiny and geopolitical friction, potentially limiting the availability of specialized heavy machinery.

India / market angle

India is currently in the midst of an aggressive infrastructure push, including metro rail, highway tunnels, and mountain passes. Dependence on Chinese-manufactured TBMs creates a strategic vulnerability; if supply is restricted, project timelines could face significant delays and increased costs, forcing India to accelerate domestic manufacturing or diversify procurement to Europe or Japan.

What to watch

Updates on the Ministry of Road Transport and Highways' procurement tenders and any policy shifts regarding import substitution for heavy construction equipment.
