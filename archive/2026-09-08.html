India economy & policy · News On AIR · read original ↗

What happened

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has decided to keep the repo rate unchanged, prioritizing inflation control while maintaining a stable growth outlook. The central bank projects real GDP growth for the current fiscal year at 6.9%, balancing domestic demand against global economic headwinds.

India / market angle

The decision to hold rates provides stability for borrowing costs, which is critical for India's domestic credit-sensitive sectors like real estate and automobiles. It also signals the RBI's focus on managing core inflation, which helps maintain the relative stability of the Indian Rupee (INR) against volatile global capital flows.

What to watch

Watch the upcoming CPI inflation data releases to see if the RBI's 'neutral' stance shifts toward a potential rate cut in the next quarter.

Furthermore, the massive trade surplus suggests a continued reliance on Chinese supply chains, which complicates India's strategic efforts to diversify its import dependencies in electronics and critical minerals.
