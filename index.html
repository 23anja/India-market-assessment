TSMC Halts Advanced Chip Shipments to Chinese Customers

China & global trade · Yahoo Finance · read original ↗

What happened

Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Co. (TSMC) has suspended shipments of its most advanced AI-focused chips to Chinese clients to comply with tightening U.S. export controls. This move follows reports that some Chinese firms were attempting to bypass sanctions by using intermediaries to access high-end processing power. The decision marks a significant escalation in the technological decoupling between the U.S. and China.

India / market angle

As India seeks to build its own semiconductor ecosystem and attract manufacturing investment, this move underscores the risks of relying on Chinese supply chains for critical technology. It may accelerate India's efforts to forge deeper semiconductor partnerships with the U.S. and Taiwan, while also highlighting the potential for India to become an alternative manufacturing hub for firms looking to de-risk from China.

What to watch

Monitor whether the U.S. Department of Commerce expands these restrictions to other foundries or if Chinese firms accelerate their domestic efforts to develop advanced lithography and chip-design capabilities.

Iranian President Describes Economic Situation as 'Silent War'

Global geopolitics · Al Jazeera · read original ↗

What happened

Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian has characterized the country's current economic struggles, exacerbated by international sanctions and regional instability, as a 'silent economic war.' The rhetoric highlights the severe pressure on Iran's currency, inflation rates, and ability to trade oil effectively on global markets. Tehran continues to face diplomatic isolation while struggling to maintain internal economic stability.

India / market angle

India has historically maintained a complex relationship with Iran, balancing energy needs with geopolitical pressures from the U.S. Persistent instability in Iran affects the viability of the Chabahar Port project, a key strategic asset for India to access Central Asia. Furthermore, any disruption to Iranian oil exports or regional shipping lanes directly impacts global oil prices, which are a primary driver of India's current account deficit and inflation.

What to watch

Watch for any changes in the enforcement of U.S. sanctions on Iranian oil exports and potential shifts in India's diplomatic stance regarding the Chabahar Port's operational status.

U.S. President-Elect Trump Cites India’s Growth to Critique Federal Reserve Policy

Global economy & central banks · Business Standard · read original ↗

What happened

President-elect Donald Trump has publicly praised India’s 7% GDP growth and relatively lower interest rates, using them as a benchmark to criticize the U.S. Federal Reserve's current monetary policy. Trump argued that the U.S. could achieve significantly higher growth rates if the Fed were not 'politicized' and kept rates lower. This commentary reflects a broader political debate in the U.S. regarding the balance between inflation control and economic expansion.

India / market angle

While the comparison is largely rhetorical for domestic U.S. political consumption, it highlights India's growing profile as a 'bright spot' in global macroeconomics. For India, this positive attention from the incoming U.S. administration may signal a favorable environment for continued trade negotiations and investment flows, though it also places pressure on the RBI to maintain stable growth-inflation dynamics to sustain this narrative.

What to watch

Watch for any formal policy shifts in the U.S. regarding the Federal Reserve's independence or interest rate trajectory, which would influence global capital flows and the strength of the U.S. dollar against the Indian Rupee.
