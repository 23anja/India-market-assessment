What happened

The Reserve Bank of India (RBI) has decided to keep the repo rate unchanged, prioritizing inflation control while maintaining a stable monetary environment. Simultaneously, the central bank has projected a robust real GDP growth of 6.9% for the current fiscal year, signaling confidence in the domestic economy's resilience despite global headwinds.

India / market angle

This decision directly influences borrowing costs for Indian consumers and businesses, impacting credit growth and capital expenditure. It also serves as a critical signal to foreign institutional investors (FIIs) regarding the stability of the Indian rupee and the central bank's commitment to price stability, which is essential for sustained equity market inflows.

What to watch

Monitor the upcoming Consumer Price Index (CPI) inflation prints and the RBI's commentary on liquidity management to gauge if a rate cut might be on the table in the next policy cycle.

What happened

India / market angle

For India, U.S. inflation data is a primary driver of FII sentiment. Higher-than-expected U.S. inflation typically strengthens the dollar, putting downward pressure on the Indian Rupee (INR) and potentially triggering capital outflows from Indian equity markets as the yield gap between U.S. Treasuries and Indian assets narrows.

What to watch

The U.S. Consumer Price Index (CPI) release and the subsequent reaction of the 10-year U.S. Treasury yield, which acts as a benchmark for global risk appetite.
